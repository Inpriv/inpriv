Law Enforcement Guidelines

For police, prosecutors, courts and other authorities requesting data about Inpriv users. Users can read this page too: we publish it so everyone knows how requests are handled.

Effective 17 October 2026

Requests go to legal@inpriv.xyz, sent from an official government email domain. Emergencies: see below. Requests in English are handled fastest.

What exists, and what doesn't

Inpriv is built so that some data never reaches us. We can only provide records we actually hold. The Privacy Policy describes all of them.

What a request must include

  • the name of the authority, and the name, rank, phone number and official email of the requesting officer;
  • the legal basis: a subpoena, court order, warrant or equivalent valid legal process, with a copy attached;
  • the exact identifiers: @inpriv.xyz addresses, or IP addresses with exact timestamps and timezone;
  • the specific records you're asking for, and the time range;
  • any non-disclosure order, and the legal basis for it.

We review every request for validity and scope. We push back on requests that are overly broad, unclear or not legally valid, and we disclose only the minimum the process requires. Requests from authorities outside the jurisdiction we're bound by may need to go through a mutual legal assistance treaty (MLAT) or a similar channel.

Emergencies

If there's an imminent risk of death or serious physical injury, write to legal@inpriv.xyz with the subject "EMERGENCY DISCLOSURE REQUEST". Describe the emergency, explain how the data will help, and include the identifiers. We may voluntarily disclose information that's needed to prevent the harm, where the law allows.

Preservation

We'll preserve specific existing records (for example access-log entries that would otherwise be deleted after 90 days) for 90 days on receiving an official preservation request, while you obtain formal legal process. We'll extend that once by another 90 days if asked. A preservation request doesn't disclose anything; it only keeps data from being deleted.

Notifying users

We tell users about requests for their data before disclosing it, so they can seek legal advice, unless:

  • the law or a court order forbids it;
  • it's an emergency;
  • notice would endanger someone or seriously harm an investigation.

When a restriction ends, we'll notify the user.

Requests we don't accept

  • Requests that don't come from an authority, or don't come through valid legal process. This includes requests from private parties, lawyers or companies for other people's data, unless the law requires us to comply.
  • Requests to decrypt content, to weaken encryption, or to add surveillance to the service. We can't decrypt end-to-end encrypted mail, and we won't build a way to.

Abuse reports

If you only want to report abuse, such as spam or phishing from an @inpriv.xyz address, and aren't requesting data, write to abuse@inpriv.xyz. We act on abuse without a legal request; see the Acceptable Use Policy.
