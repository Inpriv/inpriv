Privacy Policy

What Inpriv stores, what it can and can't read, how long it keeps things, and who else handles your data.

Effective 22 October 2026. Earlier versions are in the source history.

The short version

  • Mail between Inpriv users is end-to-end encrypted in the sender's browser. Your mailbox keys are locked with a mailbox passphrase that never leaves your device.
  • Mail from or to other providers (Gmail, Outlook…) is normal email. Our mail provider and our server can read it while it passes through; incoming mail is then stored encrypted to your key.
  • Subject lines, sender and recipient addresses, sizes and times are not encrypted. We need them to deliver and list your mail.
  • Temp inboxes are not encrypted. Their messages are deleted after 24 hours. The address itself is never given to anyone else.
  • We keep a security access log (the network your IP address belongs to, country, network operator, browser family, time, what happened) for 30 days.
  • Opening an email doesn't load anything from the sender until you choose Load images.
  • No ads, no analytics, no tracking cookies, no data sales. The code is open source, so you can check this.
  • Inpriv gives you an address that isn't your name. It doesn't make you anonymous (section 15).

1. Who we are

"Inpriv", "we" and "us" mean the operator of the Inpriv services at inpriv.xyz, mail.inpriv.xyz, temp.inpriv.xyz and id.inpriv.xyz. We are the controller of the personal data described here. Questions and requests: privacy@inpriv.xyz.

Mailboxes created before 22 October 2026

Until then, a mailbox's private key was locked with your Inpriv ID password, which our server receives every time you sign in. We never recorded it, but a compromised or compelled server could have used it to unlock that key. The next time you open such a mailbox, Mail asks you to set a mailbox passphrase:

  • your earlier key is moved into a new key ring locked with the passphrase, so your earlier mail stays readable;
  • a new key is made for mail sent to you from then on, and senders can only encrypt to that new key;
  • the copy locked with your password is removed from our servers (it may stay in Cloudflare's restorable history for up to 7 days, section 13).

This can't undo the past: mail encrypted to your earlier key was protected only as well as your password was. If that matters for some of it, delete those messages. Until you upgrade, your mailbox keeps working the old way.

Mail to and from other providers

  • Incoming: mail from other providers reaches our mail provider Resend over normal email. Our server fetches it, removes anything that could run in your browser (scripts, forms, frames), encrypts it to your public key and stores it.
  • Outgoing: mail you send to other providers is handed to Resend as normal email. Our server, Resend and the recipient's provider can read it. The copy in your Sent box is encrypted to your key.

5. Inpriv Temp

  • The inbox: its address, a hash of the access key your browser holds, and created and expiry times.
  • Messages: sender, recipient, subject, text and HTML body (with anything that could run removed), attachments (up to 512 KB each, 2 MB per message), size and time. These are stored unencrypted, so don't use Temp for anything sensitive.
  • Deletion: the inbox and its messages are deleted once it expires, 24 hours after it's created; cleanup runs every hour. Messages are never kept longer than 7 days in any case. You can delete an inbox yourself at any time.
  • Not logged: creating an inbox isn't written to the access log, and we don't record which network created it. Short-lived counters limit how many inboxes one network can create per hour.

6. Addresses are never reused

Every @inpriv.xyz address belongs to one owner at a time, and once it has been handed out it is never given to anyone else: not after a Temp inbox expires, and not after an account is deleted. Mail meant for the earlier holder, such as password resets from other sites, can't reach a new one.

To make that possible we keep, for every address ever issued, the address itself, which kind of service took it (account, Temp or team) and the day. Nothing else: no IP address, no account id, no messages. This record is kept for as long as Inpriv runs.

A limit we can't fix: before 22 October 2026, expired Temp addresses could be issued again, and most of them were never recorded. We now refuse every address in the old Temp format (like amber-fox-123) and every Temp address in our records, but a custom Temp name (one you typed yourself) that was used and expired before 17 October 2026 isn't on record, and could still be claimed.

7. Access log and security events

To investigate account takeovers and abuse reports, and to answer valid legal requests, we keep an access log. Each entry has:

  • the time, the service, and what happened, such as "Mail sign-in", "message sent" or "mailbox key upgraded";
  • the account (username);
  • the network your IP address belongs to, not the full address: the first three parts of an IPv4 address (203.0.113.0/24) or the first 48 bits of an IPv6 address;
  • the country and network operator (ASN) for that address, and your browser family and system (for example "Firefox · Linux").

It's written when you create, sign in to or change your Inpriv ID (failed sign-ins included), sign in to Mail, create or upgrade a mailbox, change your mailbox passphrase or send a message. It doesn't record recipients, message content, passwords, passphrases, keys, tokens or recovery codes. Entries are deleted after 30 days, including after you delete your account.

A network, a country and a time are not anonymous: combined with an account, they can still point to a person or a household.

Entries written before 22 October 2026 hold the full IP address, the full browser string and, for sent mail, the recipient address. They're deleted 90 days after they were written, so all of them are gone by 20 January 2027.

Separately, your security log on id.inpriv.xyz (section 3) keeps the first part of your IP address and your browser name for 90 days, so you can review your own account.

8. Images and links in email

Emails often load images from the sender's servers, which tells the sender that you opened the message, when, your IP address and your browser. Mail and Temp block all of that by default: images, tracking pixels, stylesheets, fonts and other files hosted elsewhere. Scripts, forms and embedded pages never run.

If you choose Load images for a message, your browser fetches that message's images from the sender's servers, and the sender learns the above. Opening a link takes you to the other site, which sees your IP address and browser; Inpriv doesn't pass on which page you came from.

9. On your device

We don't use tracking or advertising cookies. The sites store only what they need to work:

  • id.inpriv.xyz: a sign-in cookie that page scripts can't read (HttpOnly, Secure, SameSite=Strict). Other Inpriv sites ask id.inpriv.xyz whether you're signed in, for Quick Sign-In.
  • Mail: a sign-in cookie of the same kind, for mail.inpriv.xyz only. While Mail is open, your unlocked keys are held in the page's memory and are dropped when you sign out or close it. The page also remembers the key ids of people you've written to, to warn you if one changes.
  • Mail, with "Skip mailbox passphrase" on: a random device key and device id in the browser's storage. With them, this browser opens your mailbox without the passphrase. Anyone who can use this browser profile can open your mailbox too, and so could any script that managed to run on mail.inpriv.xyz. Use it only on devices you trust. Turning it off on any device removes it from every device; you can also forget single devices in Mail's Account tab.
  • Temp: the access key for your current inbox, in the browser's storage, until the inbox expires or you delete it.
  • Everywhere: your theme and a few display preferences.

Clearing your browser's site data removes all of this.

10. Account and key recovery

  • Inpriv ID password: there's no reset by email yet. If you have two-factor on, a recovery code replaces a lost authenticator, not a lost password.
  • Mailbox passphrase: nobody can recover it, including us: we never have it. If you lose it, you can start over with a new key (with your password, and your two-factor code if it's on). Your address keeps working and new mail can be read, but mail sent to you before stays stored and can't be opened again. Recovering your account never gives anyone your earlier mail.

11. Mail you send to us

Messages to our team addresses (support@, abuse@, dmca@, legal@, privacy@, security@, admin@, hello@) are stored as received, so we can answer them, and kept until we delete them. They aren't end-to-end encrypted.

15. Pseudonymous, not anonymous

An Inpriv address doesn't carry your name, but that alone doesn't make you anonymous. What can link an address to you:

  • the access log, which ties sign-ins to a network for 30 days, and the networks and times our providers see;
  • a recovery email, if you add one;
  • what you write, who you write to, and when: encryption doesn't hide that routing information;
  • using the same address, display name or writing style elsewhere;
  • mail to other providers, which they read and keep under their own policies.

Tor or a VPN can hide your IP address from us. They don't hide your account, your recovery email, the people you write to or what you write. If your safety depends on not being identified, don't rely on Inpriv alone.

16. Legal bases

Where the EU or UK GDPR applies, we rely on:

  • contract: your account, mailbox, messages, sessions and settings, which we need to provide the service you signed up for;
  • legitimate interests: the access log, security events, rate limits and the record of issued addresses, to keep the service and its users secure. We limit them to what's needed for that, and delete them on the schedule above.
  • legal obligation: responding to valid legal requests and keeping data a preservation request covers;
  • consent: the optional recovery email, which you can remove at any time.

17. Your rights

Depending on where you live, you can ask us to:

  • give you a copy of your data;
  • correct it;
  • delete it (you can delete your account yourself in your account settings on id.inpriv.xyz);
  • restrict or object to how we use it;
  • hand it to you in a portable format.

Write to privacy@inpriv.xyz from your @inpriv.xyz address, or say how we can verify you. We answer within one month.

We can't hand over stored mail content, because we can't decrypt it, but you can open and copy it yourself while you're signed in. You also have the right to complain to your data-protection authority.

18. Security, and its limits

  • All traffic uses HTTPS, and every page tells browsers to use only HTTPS.
  • Passwords are stored as salted hashes, 2FA secrets are encrypted at rest, and only hashes of session tokens are stored.
  • Your mailbox keys are locked with a passphrase we never receive. Mail that other people send you is encrypted to a public key you can check: Mail shows each key's id and warns you when a known recipient's key changes.
  • Pages only run their own scripts, and email content is shown in a frame that can't run any.
  • The admin panel needs a separate authenticator code; every lookup of user records in it is logged.

What encryption in a web page can't protect against: the code that encrypts your mail is delivered by our servers each time you open Mail. If those servers, or the code, were compromised or compelled to change, a modified page could capture your passphrase or messages. The same applies if your device or browser is compromised. The code is open source so changes are visible, but your browser doesn't check the code it runs against the published source.

No independent audit of Inpriv has been done. If a breach affects your data, we'll tell you and the relevant authority as the law requires. Found a vulnerability? See SECURITY.md.

19. Children

Inpriv isn't for children under 16. We don't knowingly hold data about them. If you think a child has an account, write to privacy@inpriv.xyz and we'll delete it.
